## PRINTED SOURCE (not instructor additions)
**Common series:** Kneeling Arm Forward Footbar Series. Photos show both knees on carriage and hands gripping strap handles, facing footbar. The printed subsections are 1 Straight Forward, 2 Biceps Curl (p.39), 3 Teardrop outward/inward, 4 Salute (p.40).

**p.39 Why:** Shoulder & Elbow Flexor strengthening; improve neutral posture and stability. **Starting Position:** hands grip straps; kneel on carriage facing footbar, arms extended alongside trunk (printed Korean wording specifies straightened arms beside trunk). **Breathing:** INHALE Ready → EXHALE 1 Shoulder Flexion / 2 Elbow Flexion → INHALE Return to Starting Position. **Movement Point:** Transverse Abdominis, Pelvic Floor; Neutral Spine; Scapula Stabilizer; Shoulder Flexion; Shoulder Circumduction; Elbow Flexion / Extension. **Watch Point:** avoid pelvic rocking and excessive lumbar lordosis; maintain torso upright rather than leaning forward; avoid shoulder elevation; avoid compensatory elbow movement; keep strap handles controlled. Printed watch text should be checked again for exact Korean nuance, especially elbow compensation.

**p.40 Why:** Shoulder-joint strengthening; improve neutral posture and stability. **Starting Position:** 3 and 4 kneel facing footbar with arms straight beside trunk. **Breathing:** INHALE Ready → EXHALE 3 outward/inward / 4 Elbow Extension → INHALE Return to Starting Position. **Movement Point:** Transverse Abdominis, Pelvic Floor; Neutral Spine; Scapula Stabilizer; Shoulder Flexion; Shoulder Circumduction; Elbow Flexion / Extension. **Variations:** Sitting Position (1,3,4); place elbows on footbar; Sitting Position – Offering (Elbow Flexion – Starting Position), EXHALE Elbow Extension, INHALE Shoulder Horizontal Abduction, EXHALE Shoulder Horizontal Adduction, INHALE Return to Starting Position. Printed text does **not** specify independent breaths for every photographed intermediate phase.

## INSTRUCTOR SUPPLEMENT / BIOMECHANICAL INTERPRETATION (not printed as source)
Suggested focus: rib-pelvis alignment and gentle abdominal bracing without forced posterior pelvic tilt; stabilize scapulae while allowing normal upward rotation during arm elevation; avoid pushing through shoulder pain. Likely contributors include anterior deltoid for shoulder flexion, biceps/brachialis for elbow flexion, triceps for elbow extension, serratus anterior and trapezius for scapular control; **these are anatomical interpretations, not measured activation or a printed muscle list**. Do not infer that the straps or hands must be at one exact angle. Use a tolerable spring and range for the individual.
